The 4 Classic APUSH Multiple-Choice Traps

Every incorrect option ("distractor") on an AP US History test is designed using one of these patterns. Learn to spot them and cross them out immediately:

Trap 1: Out of Period (Chronology Trap)

The statement is historically true, but happened in the wrong era (e.g., mentioning the Nullification Crisis of 1832 or Bacon's Rebellion of 1676 on a question about 1765). Fix: Check the date in the source attribution line first!

Trap 2: Reversed Causation

Flips cause and effect (e.g., claiming the Proclamation of 1763 caused the French and Indian War, rather than the war causing the Proclamation).

Trap 3: Extreme / Absolute Language

Uses words like "completely eliminated," "universal support," or "all colonists agreed." History is complex—correct AP answers use measured language.

Trap 4: Wrong Theme / Category

The question asks about an economic motive or political debate, and the choice gives a true religious fact that doesn't answer the prompt.

Unit 3 Chronological Anchor Timeline (1754–1800)

1754–1763
Seven Years' War (French & Indian War)

Albany Plan of Union fails; Britain expels France (Treaty of Paris 1763) but incurs massive war debt → End of Salutary Neglect.

1763
Pontiac's Rebellion & Proclamation of 1763

Native confederacy resists British expansion; Crown bans colonial settlement west of Appalachians, angering colonists.

1764–1773
Imperial Taxation & Colonial Resistance

Sugar Act, Stamp Act (1765), Townshend Acts, Tea Act. Colonists respond with boycotts (nonimportation), Sons/Daughters of Liberty, and arguments against "virtual representation."

1776
Common Sense & Declaration of Independence

Thomas Paine attacks monarchy; Jefferson applies Locke's natural rights & social contract theory. Crucial French alliance follows Battle of Saratoga (1777).

1781–1787
Articles of Confederation & Shays' Rebellion

Weak central government (no power to tax or regulate commerce; no executive). Success: Northwest Ordinance of 1787 (banned slavery in NW Territory, path to statehood). Shays' Rebellion exposes federal weakness.

1787–1791
Constitution, Ratification & Bill of Rights

Great Compromise, 3/5 Compromise. Federalist Papers (Hamilton/Madison) vs. Anti-Federalists (demanded Bill of Rights to protect individual liberties).

1789–1800
First Party System & Early Republic

Hamilton's Financial Plan (Assumption, National Bank, Tariffs, Whiskey Tax) sparks split between Federalists (Hamilton/Adams) and Democratic-Republicans (Jefferson/Madison). Washington's Farewell Address warns against permanent foreign alliances and political factions. Alien & Sedition Acts (1798) prompt Virginia & Kentucky Resolutions.
